Budget proposal for financial year 2026

To: the Executive Committee of Wintermoor Retail GmbH
From: Henry Ashdown, Finance
Date: 10 July 2026
Restricted circulation. Draft figures, not yet approved.

Overview
This memorandum sets out the proposed budget for next year. We are planning for income of €117,000 and a surplus of €97,500, which is 10% of income. The plan is cautious: it assumes no new product launches and only those price changes that have already been announced to customers.

Income assumptions
Subscription income is budgeted to grow by 10%, driven by renewals at the new list price. Consulting income is held flat. We have assumed that churn stays at this year's level and that the two largest accounts renew on existing terms. A one-point rise in churn would reduce income by roughly €1,300.

Payroll
Payroll is the largest line, at €89,500. The budget funds a general pay rise of three per cent from April, nine new positions phased across the year, and employer social security contributions at current rates. The bonus pool is set at €80,500 and is paid only if the surplus target is reached.

Other operating costs
Cloud hosting rises in line with usage, to €118,000. Rent is fixed under the lease. Travel is cut by a fifth compared with this year. Marketing is concentrated in the first half, around two trade fairs. A contingency of €650 is held centrally and released only by decision of the finance director.

Capital investment
We propose capital spending of €80,500: replacement laptops on the four-year cycle, a refit of the second-floor meeting rooms and the first phase of the new billing system. The billing project is expected to pay back within three years through lower write-offs and faster month-end closing.

Cash and funding
On these figures cash never falls below €97,500 during the year, with the low point in August, when annual licences are paid. No new borrowing is needed. The existing credit line stays in place as a precaution and its commitment fee is included in finance costs.

Sensitivities
We tested three downside cases: a delay of one quarter in hiring, the loss of the largest account, and a ten per cent rise in hosting prices. Only the loss of the largest account turns the surplus into a deficit, and in that case the bonus pool and the room refit would be withdrawn.

Decisions requested
The committee is asked to approve the income and cost budgets, to approve capital spending subject to a separate business case for the billing system, and to confirm the surplus target that governs the bonus pool. Department heads will receive their allocations within a week of approval, and monthly reporting against budget starts in February.

Henry Ashdown
Finance
