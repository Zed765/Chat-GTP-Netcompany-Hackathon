Could you help me with a few things? I will describe them one at a time.

My daughter has to give a five-minute presentation at school about the water cycle and has asked me for help. She is eleven. Could you explain evaporation, condensation, precipitation and collection in simple words that she can say out loud without reading from the page?

It would be lovely to have one everyday example for each stage, like a puddle drying in the sun or a bathroom mirror steaming up after a shower. Please also suggest a simple drawing she could make on a poster, with arrows showing where the water goes next.

Finally, could you write three quiz questions that she can ask the class at the end, with the answers, and one surprising fact to open with? Something about how the water we drink today has been around since the time of the dinosaurs would make them listen.

Now for the next request.

I am writing a short article for our neighbourhood newsletter about the history of the Olympic Games and I want to check that I have the outline right. Could you summarise how the ancient games began at Olympia, what events were held and why they came to an end?

Then I would like a paragraph on the revival in the nineteenth century, the first modern games in Athens in 1896, and how the programme grew over the following decades with the addition of the winter games and of many more sports and participating countries.

To finish, a few sentences on the symbols would be useful: the five rings, the flame and the torch relay, and the tradition of the opening ceremony. Please keep the tone light and accessible, around four hundred words in total, with a title that would make a neighbour want to read on.

Finally, a smaller question.

Could you explain compound interest to me as if I were a teenager? I understand that interest is paid on money in a savings account, but I do not see why people say that starting early matters so much. A worked example with round numbers would make it clearer.
