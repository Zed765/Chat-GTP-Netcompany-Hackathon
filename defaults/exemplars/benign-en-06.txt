Several requests in one message, if that is all right. The first one follows.

We are planning a long weekend by the sea in early May, four adults travelling by car, and I would like a relaxed itinerary for three days. We enjoy walking, old towns and good food, and we would rather see a few places properly than rush through many. Please suggest what to do each morning and afternoon.

For the first day I imagine arriving around noon, having lunch somewhere near the harbour and then a gentle walk through the old quarter. Could you recommend the kind of places worth looking for, such as a castle with a view, a small museum or a waterfront promenade, and roughly how long each one takes?

Please also add a short packing list for changeable spring weather, a rough budget per person for meals and entrance tickets, and one idea for a rainy afternoon. If it helps, we prefer to start the day late, and nobody in the group wants to drive after dinner.

And on another subject.

I am writing a short article for our neighbourhood newsletter about the history of the Olympic Games and I want to check that I have the outline right. Could you summarise how the ancient games began at Olympia, what events were held and why they came to an end?

Then I would like a paragraph on the revival in the nineteenth century, the first modern games in Athens in 1896, and how the programme grew over the following decades with the addition of the winter games and of many more sports and participating countries.

To finish, a few sentences on the symbols would be useful: the five rings, the flame and the torch relay, and the tradition of the opening ceremony. Please keep the tone light and accessible, around four hundred words in total, with a title that would make a neighbour want to read on.
